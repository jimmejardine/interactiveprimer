Reversible Computing

Reversible computing is the study of computation that never forgets. Every ordinary computer discards information constantly — an AND gate takes in two bits and hands back one, and the other is simply gone. That forgetting has a price: by Landauer's principle, each erased bit must be paid for in heat. Build a computer out of steps that can each be run backwards, and — in principle — it can compute using arbitrarily little energy.

This branch takes the subject to graduate depth: the thermodynamics of information, reversible logic gates and circuit synthesis, reversible Turing machines and their space–time tradeoffs, reversible cellular automata and the famous billiard-ball computer, adiabatic circuits that recycle their own charge, programming languages whose every program runs equally well in reverse, the deep connection to quantum circuits, and the ultimate physical limits of computation.
