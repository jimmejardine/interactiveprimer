Chip Design

Chip design is the craft of turning an idea into silicon: describing hardware in a language, proving it correct, squeezing it through synthesis and place-and-route, and shipping a die with billions of transistors that all switch on the first try. It is the discipline behind every processor you own — and this branch teaches it the honest way: by building things. You will write real register-transfer-level SystemVerilog, verify it like an industry team, follow a design through the FPGA and ASIC flows all the way to tape-out, construct a working RISC-V CPU, wire it into a system-on-chip — and then spend the back half of the course on the most remarkable chips of our era: GPUs, from warp schedulers and rasterizers to tensor cores and chiplets, finishing with a minimal GPU you can watch execute a kernel cycle by cycle.
